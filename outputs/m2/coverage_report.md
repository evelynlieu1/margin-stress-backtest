## 1. Coverage at 2 days (non-overlapping moves)

A long breaches when the move is below -margin, a short when it is above +margin. Kupiec p tests 'breach rate = 1%'; Christoffersen p tests 'breaches do not cluster' (n/a = too few breaches to test); CC p tests both together. 'rate % all days' is the breach rate over every day's 2-day move: the best estimate, the same whatever the start day. The tests need independent moves, so they use every 2nd day: n, breaches, 'rate %' and the p-values are for the sample starting on the first day; 'Kupiec fails', 'CC passes' and 'clustered' say on how many of the 2 possible start days Kupiec p is below 0.05, CC p is 0.05 or more, and Christoffersen p is below 0.05.

## Limitations

- Spot prices stand in for futures. Dated Brent came apart from futures from 30 Mar to May 2020 (-23% on 31 Mar, +35% on 2 Apr, $9.12 on 21 Apr); the EWMA volatility carries that shock for weeks after, so Brent margins into mid-2020 are higher than a futures margin would have been.
- 16 rule-side tests at 2 days and 32 more at 1 and 5 days, with no correction for multiple testing: a few 'fails' at p just under 0.05 are expected by chance.
- No margin-period buffer, concentration add-on or liquidity add-on, which real clearing houses use; and margins here are recomputed every day.
- R3 scales a 1-day distribution by sqrt(h), which ignores any momentum or mean reversion over 2-5 days.
- At 2 days there are about 2,479 test periods per series and side, so about 24 breaches are expected: the tests cannot reliably tell 1% from 1.3%.
- Margins are in log terms. A long margin of m covers a price fall of 1 - e^-m (10 log % = 9.5%); above about 70 log % (April 2020 Brent) the gap to a plain % is large.
